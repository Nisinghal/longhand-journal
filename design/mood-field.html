The mood field

Twenty-five colours, generated rather than picked. Every entry lands somewhere on a grid of how the day sat against how much you had in you, and that coordinate becomes the day's colour for the rest of its life.

Most mood apps hand you five faces on a traffic light: red is bad, green is good, and you are being graded. That reads clinical, and it flattens the difference between a heavy day you had energy for and a heavy day you slept through.

So mood is two dimensions here. Valence moves the hue along a dawn arc — night indigo, blue hour, sea green, morning amber. Energy moves saturation and weight: a spent day is barely tinted paper, a charged day is dense with pigment. No red anywhere. Nothing is scored.

How a colour gets made

All twenty-five come out of two ramps in OKLCH, which keeps them perceptually even — no single swatch shouts louder than its neighbours the way it would in HSL.

Energy → chroma + lightness

SpentSlowSteadyLivelyCharged
hue   = [268, 243, 152, 85, 40][valence]     // indigo → apricot
paper L = 0.930 − 0.0260·energy   C = 0.014 + 0.0150·energy
night L = 0.340 + 0.0520·energy   C = 0.016 + 0.0145·energy

colour = oklch(L C hue)

Chroma tops out at 0.074. That ceiling is the whole character of the thing — high enough that twenty-five swatches stay distinct side by side, low enough that no single day ever looks like an alert.

Two ramps, one function, and the whole palette regenerates if we shift the arc later. It lives in src/theme/moodColor.ts.
